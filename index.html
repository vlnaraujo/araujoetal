About

Who I am

I earned my Biology degree from the Federal University of Pernambuco. Currently I am a PhD researcher from the Universidad de los Andes (Colombia) dedicated to understanding the mechanisms behind the diversification of Neotropical fauna.

My research integrates multiple approaches: phylogenetic inference, population genomics, museomics, comparative methods, and historical biogeography. Together, these tools let me reconstruct the evolutionary history of lineages and ask what forces shaped the patterns of diversity we see today.

I am particularly interested in how geological and climatic changes in the Neotropics have driven speciation across taxonomic groups — though I am very much open to working in other realms as well.

Research

What I research

01

Phylogenetics

Infering the evolutionary relationships with molecular and morphological data to understand their diversification.

02

Genomics

Using population and comparative genomics to uncover the genetic basis of divergence and adaptation across taxa.

03

Museomics

Extracting genomic data from historical museum specimens to access genetic resources from extinct and rare species.

04

Biogeography

Exploring how geography, geology, and climate have shaped the spatial distribution of biodiversity across the Neotropics.

05

Comparative Biology

Using comparative methods to understand trait evolution and ecological adaptation across lineages.
