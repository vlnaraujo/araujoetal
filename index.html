About

Who I am

I am a biologist and PhD researcher dedicated to understanding the mechanisms behind the diversification of Neotropical fauna.

My research integrates multiple approaches: phylogenetic inference, population genomics, museomics, comparative methods, and historical biogeography. Together, these tools let me reconstruct the evolutionary history of lineages and ask what forces shaped the patterns of diversity we see today.

I am particularly interested in how geological and climatic changes in the Neotropics have driven speciation across taxonomic groups (But I am very much open to work with other realms).

Research

What I study

01

Phylogenetics

Reconstructing the evolutionary relationships of Neotropical lineages using molecular and morphological data to understand their diversification.

02

Genomics

Leveraging population and comparative genomics to uncover the genetic basis of divergence and adaptation across taxa.

03

Museomics

Extracting genomic data from historical museum specimens to access genetic diversity across time and track evolutionary change.

04

Biogeography

Exploring how geography, geology, and climate have shaped the spatial distribution of biodiversity across the Neotropics.

05

Comparative Biology

Using comparative methods to understand trait evolution, and ecological adaptation across lineages.
